Reading the three eyes

Eye 1 is Primary, eye 2 Secondary and eye 3 Tertiary. Size and numbering distinguish their rank without relying on color alone. Blue → yellow → red indicates low → high relative saliency. The ranks compare distinct candidate regions within this image; they are not fixation probabilities. Flat or transparent images show no eyes. Fewer than three qualifying regions produce fewer markers.

Research and adaptation

Itti, Koch and Niebur (1998) combine multiscale intensity, color and orientation contrast, then select locations with inhibition of return. Achanta et al. (2009) compare Gaussian-smoothed CIELAB colors with the image mean. The SaliencyToolbox GitHub implementation was inspected for its center–surround calculation. Color Atlas uses these ideas in an original browser algorithm; no repository code is bundled.

This is a lightweight adaptation, not either published model: OKLab replaces CIELAB for the global-color term; edge magnitude replaces orientation-selective Gabor filters; the scale construction, alpha handling, weights and focus extraction are app choices. It runs locally without downloading model weights or uploading images.

Image and feature calculation

The completed preview is read after bloom and the selected color-vision filter, before the heatmap and eyes. With a vision comparison active, analysis uses the complete filtered image, independent of divider position; the Regular Vision side covers the overlay in that portion. Move the divider left to inspect all filtered-image foci. Area averaging reduces the image to at most 96 × 96 cells. Decode sRGB before averaging. Composite each pixel over a fixed neutral ground of 0.18 linear RGB; CSS backgrounds are excluded; backgrounds baked into the image are analyzed. The bundled reference is opaque and includes a baked-in checkerboard, which can influence scores. This makes partially transparent colors depend on a stated background and prevents hidden RGB from affecting the estimate.

RGB_ground = alpha × decode_sRGB(RGB) + (1 − alpha) × 0.18
Y = 0.2126 R_ground + 0.7152 G_ground + 0.0722 B_ground
(L, a, b) = OKLab(RGB_ground)

Each channel is smoothed with separable [1, 4, 6, 4, 1] / 16 filters, replicated at image boundaries. Five full-grid levels use an initial step of 1, followed by steps 1, 2, 4 and 8. Compare level pairs (0, 2), (1, 3) and (2, 4), averaging each feature over the three pairs.

I = mean |Y_center − Y_surround|
C = mean hypot(a_center − a_surround, b_center − b_surround)
E = mean |edge(Y_center) − edge(Y_surround)|
G = distance(smoothed OKLab, mean image OKLab)
edge(Y) = hypot(horizontal difference, vertical difference) / 2

Multiply each feature by cell alpha, normalize it separately by its maximum and discard maps below numerical contrast floors: 0.001 for I/E and 0.002 for C/G. Independent normalization retains chromatic contrast that brightness alone misses. The global mean includes the neutral ground.

S = 0.35 normalize(I) + 0.35 normalize(C)
  + 0.15 normalize(E) + 0.15 normalize(G)

S is blurred once, weighted by cell alpha again, normalized by its peak, then expanded to the original image grid. These weights, floors and transparency attenuation are explicit design parameters, not fitted or validated against human fixations. Tiny details below the proxy resolution can disappear.

Selecting distinct foci

Select the strongest visible local maximum. Suppress its connected region above 55% of that peak and a disk of radius 12% of the shorter image dimension before selecting again. Stop after three foci, or when a remaining peak is below 20% of the first peak. Candidates require alpha of at least 26/255. Connected regions use eight neighbors; local-maximum neighborhoods scale with image size. Equal scores use deterministic scan order.

This spatial suppression adapts the idea of inhibition of return; it is not a neural timing or scanpath simulation. The eyes are inset slightly at image edges so their symbols remain readable. Nearby or connected responses can count as a single focus.

What the estimate can tell you

Compare whether a palette shifts emphasis toward color boundaries, bright or dark contrast, or unusual regions. Bloom and vision filters can change the ranking. It omits semantics, faces, text, motion, task goals, display size, viewing distance and observer differences. No eye-tracking dataset or natural-image benchmark has validated this VFX adaptation, so added features do not establish improved human-gaze accuracy. The fixed neutral ground also differs from a game scene background.

Attention remains a preview aid. It does not change gradient keys, ColorPlay scoring, saved palettes or exports.
