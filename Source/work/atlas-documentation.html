Color Atlas / Start here

Quick start

Color Atlas builds gradient maps for VFX: color ramps that recolor a grayscale signal such as smoke density, particle age or a mask. You author the ramp, judge it on real images in the Preview, then export it as a texture or as engine data.

The one idea

A gradient map is a lookup. A value t between 0 and 1 picks a position on the ramp, and the ramp returns the color at that position. Black pixels of the source read the left end, white pixels read the right end. In an engine, t can be anything normalized: a texture channel, particle age, distance or a dynamic parameter.

Pause over a control, or focus it with the keyboard, for a short explanation of what it does.

From preset to texture

  1. Start from a preset. Search the library on the left and click a preset. It replaces the ramp of the selected image layer.
  2. Select a key. Click a row in the key list, or a marker under the Gradient Map.
  3. Change its color. Click or drag in the color wheel, use the lightness and color sliders, or type a HEX and press Apply.
  4. Shape the ramp. Drag markers along the Gradient Map or set Position. + adds a key, − removes the selected one.
  5. Judge it in context. The Preview updates live. Switch Smooth/Bands, toggle layers, try Random.
  6. Export. Press Export PNG, choose PNG, Unity C# or Unreal JSON, then download or copy.

In the examples, the highlighted controls are yours to try. Follow the goal above each illustration, then drag sliders, choose a mode, or pick a color. Reset example restores that example without changing your work in the Editor.

Prefer a guided tour? Press Tutorial at the top right for a short hands-on tour in three steps. Lightweight highlights and brief effects keep the tour responsive while you try the controls. The final card points you back to Documentation for interactive examples and engine setup. New here? Read Editing color and Gradients next, then Export and engine setup before you bring a texture into Unreal or Unity.

Editing color

Colors are edited in OKLab: one lightness axis and two color axes. You can change hue and strength while lightness stays put, which keeps the value structure of a ramp under control.

The controls

ControlMeaning
L (Black–White)Perceived lightness, 0–100.
Green–Red, Blue–YellowThe two OKLab color axes (shown ×250). Together they set hue and chroma.
SaturationChroma relative to the strongest sRGB color at the same lightness and hue (with Lock base to sRGB on; off, relative to a fixed chroma of 100). Not HSV saturation.
Color wheelA continuous color surface through OKLab at the selected key’s lightness: yellow up, blue down, green-cyan left, red-magenta right. Distance from the center is chroma. Picks are always fitted into sRGB.
3D volumeA faint sRGB cloud with a continuous color surface at the selected key’s lightness, arranged like a Munsell color tree (below). Drag the selection circle to edit live. Rotation is off by default.
Lock base to sRGBOn: sliders stop at the sRGB boundary. Off: sliders may request colors outside it, and the stored HEX is clipped.

Munsell Color Method: Hue, Value and Chroma

Munsell inspired the spatial organization of our 3D color graph: lightness rises vertically, hue moves around the neutral axis, and chroma grows outward. Munsell writes these as H V/C, such as 5R 5/6. Color Atlas measures its main graph in OKLab.

From Munsell to our OKLab graph

Munsell ideaIn Color Atlas
Value: dark to lightHeight is OKLab L, on our Black–White slider.
Hue: color family around the axisThe angle in the a/b plane identifies hue; the wheel shows a slice at one L.
Chroma: distance from grayDistance from the central neutral axis is OKLab chroma.

This is a shared organizing idea, not a numeric conversion. On the bundled samples:

In our graph, hue and chroma are the polar reading of OKLab, called OKLCh. The changing outer shape follows the sRGB gamut.

Reading the 3D view

The 3D view is a color solid: every color your screen can show, placed the way Albert Munsell organized color in his color tree, but measured with OKLab.

  • Up and down is lightness. Black at the bottom, white at the top, grays on the vertical axis.
  • Around the axis is hue. Red, yellow, green and blue sit at different angles; a turn around the axis walks the color wheel.
  • Outward is chroma. Colors grow stronger the farther they are from the gray axis.
  • The shape is the limit of sRGB. It bulges where a screen can show strong colors (bright yellows, dark blues) and narrows near black and white.
  • Your ramp is the dashed path. Numbered circles are your keys (1 is the rightmost key). A path that climbs steadily reads clearly in an effect; a path that zigzags in height makes bands of light and dark.

Drag the selected circle to recolor that key. Drag elsewhere to rotate, Shift-drag always rotates, middle-drag pans and the mouse wheel zooms; Reset view returns to the default angle.

What this means for ramps

  • Let lightness carry the structure. Fire, plasma and most emissive ramps read best when L rises steadily from left to right.
  • Hue and lightness are linked by the display. Vivid yellows exist only near the top of L, vivid blues and purples only lower down. Plan hue shifts with that in mind.
  • Near black and white, color collapses. Saturation can become unavailable there; that is the sRGB gamut, not a bug.

Numbers in the UI are OKLab scaled for readability (L ×100, a and b ×250). They are not CIE L*a*b* values, and distances are not ΔE.

Color Atlas / Color

Gradients: keys, Smooth and Bands

A ramp is a list of color keys at positions from 0 to 100%. Smooth blends between neighboring keys; Bands holds each key’s color until the next one.

Keys

  • Each ramp has 2–16 color keys. Key numbers count from the bright, right end: 1 is the rightmost key.
  • Click empty ramp space to add a key, drag a marker to move it, or set Position for exact placement.
  • Ramps also carry alpha keys. They are kept when you save and export, but are not edited in this workspace; new ramps are fully opaque.
  • Two keys at the same position make a hard edge, even in Smooth.

Smooth vs Bands

ModeUse it for
SmoothContinuous gradients: fire, smoke, energy, falloff. Interpolated in OKLab, so lightness and hue change evenly.
BandsToon and cel looks, stepped UI states, posterized effects. Export with point (nearest) filtering to keep hard steps.

Export can blend Smooth in OKLab (matches the editor) or in linear RGB (matches engines that cannot do OKLab). See Export and engine setup.

Color Atlas / Preview

Preview layers

The Preview converts each image to grayscale, recolors it with that image’s own Gradient Map and stacks the results like layers in a compositor. Select a layer row to edit its ramp.

How an image becomes a signal

Imported images are fitted into 512 × 512 and converted to grayscale (Rec. 709 weights on the encoded values). Brightness 0 reads the left end of the ramp, 255 the right end. Image alpha is kept and multiplies the ramp’s alpha.

Normalize values

Many renders never reach pure black or white, so they only use part of the ramp. Normalize values (per layer, off by default) stretches the darkest visible pixel to 0 and the brightest to 255. Turning it off restores the original values.

The Default scene uses the library Fire ramp for Explosion_Emissive in Add, including a black point at 0% and a dark ember at 7.2%.

Blend modes

Blending runs in linear light, so results can differ slightly from Photoshop, which blends encoded values. Add clamps at full white.

Importance 01 and 02

Every image stores two ramps, Importance 01 and Importance 02. The editor always edits the active one, Random can recolor both, and export can write both as two rows of one texture.

Preview presets and navigation

Preview presets save the whole scene (see Saving your work). Loading one recenters the view. Zoom with the wheel (1–400%), pan with middle-drag, and press Reset view to fit the image again.

Color Atlas / Preview

Presets and Random

Presets reproduce authored ramps; Random invents new ones for the layers you switched ON. Both are starting points: keep what works and refine it by hand.

The library

The bundled library has 1086 presets in seven groups (Effects, Nature & materials, Color studies, Data & diagnostics, Art studies, Design studies, Color & experiments). Search matches names, tags and folder names. A preset replaces the ramp of the selected layer; the star adds it to Favorites.

Random

Press Random to recolor every layer whose chip is ON. Key count and alpha stay. Most modes keep key positions; Perceptual respaces keys along its path. Graphic Design - Smooth and Graphic Design - Bands apply their named interpolation when Random is pressed. Selecting a mode leaves ramps unchanged. Other modes apply Smooth. Guided modes look at how much of the image each layer covers: the largest layer gets the dominant hue family, smaller ones the supporting and accent families, and — except in Free and Graphic Design — each key keeps its luminance so the value structure survives.

ColorPlay is inspired by Josef Albers’ exercises on how surroundings change a color. Its scoring is a Color Atlas heuristic, not an Albers rule.

Color Atlas / Preview

Analysis tools

Bloom, color-vision simulation and the attention overlay help you judge an effect. They change only the Preview — never the ramp or the export.

Processing order: gradient mapping → layer blending → bloom → color-vision filter → attention overlay.

Bloom

The Post-process bloom slider (0–700%) adds glow to areas whose linear luminance passes about 0.45, with a soft knee so edges do not outline. Use it to check how a ramp will feel under an engine’s bloom; your engine’s bloom, exposure and tone mapping will still differ.

Color-vision simulation

The vision menu simulates protan, deutan and tritan deficiencies (full and 50% severity) and a grayscale check. A split handle compares regular and simulated views. The goal: important structure should survive even when some hues merge.

Attention overlay

The eye toggle marks up to three regions that stand out, numbered 1–3, from a mix of brightness contrast, color contrast and edges. Use it to check that the eye lands on the core of the effect rather than a stray hot spot. It is a fast heuristic, not eye tracking: it ignores motion, faces, text and the game scene around the effect.

Color Atlas / Export

Export and engine setup

Export PNG writes the ramp as a lookup texture, or copies key data for Unity or Unreal. The texture holds only the ramp — not the image, bloom or other preview filters.

The export dialog

OptionChoices
FormatPNG texture (downloaded) · Unity C# · Unreal JSON (copied after review).
LayersActive layer, or both. PNG stacks Importance 01 above Importance 02.
Smooth interpolationPerceptual (OKLab), the default, matches the editor. Linear RGB for engines that blend RGB. Bands is unaffected.
Width / Height per layerPNG only. Width 1–4096, height 1–1024 per layer. Default 256 × 16.
AlphaRamp alpha is written as is (straight, not premultiplied).
File namePNG only; invalid characters are removed.

Sampling the texture

Sample at texel centers so 0 and 1 hit the end colors exactly:

u = (t · (W − 1) + 0.5) / W
v = 0.5                 one layer
v = 0.25 / 0.75         Importance 01 / 02 (top-left origin)

Unreal Engine 5

  1. Texture asset: sRGB on · Compression Settings UserInterface2D (RGBA) to avoid block-compression banding · Mip Gen Settings NoMipmaps · X and Y tiling Clamp · Filter Nearest for Bands, default bilinear for Smooth.
  2. Material: drive a TextureSample with UV = (u, v). Feed u from your scalar — a mask channel, Particle Relative Time, a Dynamic Parameter — remapped with the formula above.
  3. Niagara: drive the scalar per particle (for example Normalized Age, which the material reads as Particle Relative Time), or rebuild a color curve from the JSON keys.
  4. JSON: descriptive key data, not a .uasset. A CurveLinearColor blends in linear RGB, so it only matches a Linear RGB export; for the OKLab look, use the PNG.

Unity

Copy Unity C# builds Gradient objects. Perceptual uses GradientMode.PerceptualBlend with ColorSpace.Linear (Unity 6); Linear RGB uses Blend, Bands uses Fixed. Unity gradients hold at most 8 color and 8 alpha keys, so larger ramps fall back to JSON without dropping keys. For the PNG: sRGB on, Wrap Mode Clamp, mipmaps off, Point filter for Bands.

Decode sRGB exactly once

Still different after that? Check compression, tone mapping, exposure, filtering and the blend mode of the material. Perceptual export matches the editor’s math, not every pixel of your final frame.

Troubleshooting

Most surprises come from the selected layer, the signal being mapped or the texture settings in the engine. Check those before changing colors.

SymptomCheck
My palettes or presets are goneSame browser, same profile, same file path? Clearing site data or moving the HTML starts a new library. Import your last library file to bring them back.
My palettes is fullThe 8 KiB budget is used. Export what you need, then delete unused entries.
A preset changed only one layerPresets apply to the selected layer. Select another row to edit it.
Random changed nothingEvery layer is OFF. Switch the chip ON for the layers Random may recolor.
Ramp uses only part of the colorsThe source has low contrast. Turn on Normalize values for that layer.
A saturated color shiftedIt was outside sRGB and got fitted or clipped. See Editing color.
Smooth looks steppedBands is selected, or the texture is narrow, compressed or point-filtered.
PNG looks different in the enginesRGB decoded once? Compression off? Linear RGB export when the engine blends RGB? See Export and engine setup.
Two layers appear swappedThe engine flips v. Sample v = 0.75 for Importance 01 or flip the coordinate.
Download PNG does nothingThe browser blocked the download; check its download permissions and folder.
Copy HEX does not pasteLook for the “Copied #HEX” message. If automatic copy fails, a Copy manually dialog opens with the text selected.
3D picking fails at an angleThe slice is edge-on. Shift-drag to tilt it.

Isolate the difference

  1. Set Regular Vision, turn off the eye and set bloom to 0.
  2. Export a Perceptual PNG and compare its colors with the editor.
  3. Re-enable bloom, vision and layers one at a time to find which step changes the look.

Technical reference

Formulas used by the app

sRGB transfer

linear(c) = c / 12.92                    c ≤ 0.04045
          = ((c + 0.055) / 1.055)^2.4    otherwise
sRGB(x)   = 12.92 x                      x ≤ 0.0031308
          = 1.055 x^(1/2.4) − 0.055      otherwise

OKLab scaling and Smooth interpolation

L_UI = 100 L,  a_UI = 250 a,  b_UI = 250 b
w    = (t − t₀) / (t₁ − t₀)
q(t) = (1 − w) OKLab(key₀) + w OKLab(key₁)
RGB  = clamp(OKLab→linear sRGB(q(t)), 0, 1)        alpha interpolates separately

Gamut fitting and Saturation

fit: largest k ∈ [0, 1] with linearRGB(L, k·a, k·b) in [0, 1]³   (32-step binary search)
C = √(a_UI² + b_UI²)
S = clamp(100 · C / C_ref, 0, 100)%     C_ref = fitted chroma of a radius-100 point at the same L and hue
Lock off:  C_ref = 100

Grayscale signal and Normalize values

g = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B        (encoded bytes)
Normalize: v = round(255 (g − g_min) / (g_max − g_min))
t = v / 255,   output alpha = ramp alpha × image alpha

Layer blending (linear light, source-over)

Multiply  b·s          Screen  b + s − b·s          Add  min(1, b + s)
Overlay   2 b s  if b ≤ 0.5,  else 1 − 2 (1 − b)(1 − s)
α = αs + αb (1 − αs)
C = [(1 − αs) αb Cb + αs ((1 − αb) Cs + αb Blend(Cb, Cs))] / α

Bloom highlight

Y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B          (linear)
z = clamp(Y − 0.45 + 0.15, 0, 0.30)
h = min(1, max(Y − 0.45, z² / 0.60) / max(Y, 10⁻⁶))
blur radii 10 px and 3 px at 512 px, screen-blended on the encoded sRGB image at B and 0.7 B

Munsell samples

renotation xyY (Illuminant C, 2°) → Bradford C → D65 → linear sRGB, shown if in [0, 1]³
neutrals: Y = 0.975 · Newhall (1943) Value polynomial

Color vision

RGB_sim = clamp(M_mode · RGB_linear, 0, 1)        Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) matrices

Attention

S = 0.35 intensity contrast + 0.35 color contrast + 0.15 edge contrast + 0.15 global color distance
foci: strongest peaks, suppressing 12% of the shorter side around each; stop at 3 or below 20% of the first

PNG sampling

t = x / (W − 1);  RGB byte = round(255 · sRGB(ramp(t)));  alpha byte = round(255 · α(t));  row repeated H times per layer
